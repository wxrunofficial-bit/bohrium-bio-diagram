# Scientific visual design and final review

- **Scientific story and role of evidence.** Michael Alley's assertion–evidence method centers body slides on a testable, concise sentence and uses the main visual to substantiate it. For a graphical abstract, the same idea becomes a single scientific takeaway and a figure that a reader can understand quickly. Remove repetitions between title, image, and caption. [Penn State research and examples](https://writing.engr.psu.edu/research.html).
- **Typography and visual hierarchy.** For an academic talk at 16:9, start at 32–42 pt for body-slide headlines, 17–24 pt for nearby labels and 11–14 pt for citations and light captions. For a figure, set dimensions and verify type at the final printed size. Pick a high-contrast background/foreground pair and one accent motivated by the data or venue. Use identity consistently across panels. Reserve emphasis for the relevant mark. Preserve supplied institutional templates. [Anthropic PPTX design guidance](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/pptx/SKILL.md).
- **Scientific integrity.** Keep source figures proportional, retain scale, marks, units and uncertainty. Include accessible palettes, directly labeled series or redundant markers, editable text, and original table or record links. Programmatic checks should catch actual mark shifts, unavailable fonts and raster exports that cannot be edited. Avoid visually implying a causal or quantitative relationship beyond the data. [Nature research figure guide](https://research-figure-guide.nature.com/figures/preparing-figures-our-specifications/) · [K-Dense Scientific Visualization](https://github.com/K-Dense-AI/scientific-agent-skills/blob/main/skills/scientific-visualization/SKILL.md).
- **Graphical abstract.** Compose for the actual journal size and review at thumbnail and normal scale. Use the main result as a focal point, keep sufficient hierarchy between panels, communicate the relationship through meaningful position or accurate comparison, and signal speculative connections visually. [Cell Press graphical abstract guidelines](https://crosstalk.cell.com/hubfs/Files/GA_guide.pdf).

## When looking at the rendered result

Locate the main claim without reading notes. Trace mark or arrow to its intended label. Zoom each dense feature. Look for truncation, confusing collisions, unreadable contrast, unintended empty canvas and overfull areas. Correct the layout and render again. Validate editable groups/objects independently. A successful PDF export cannot prove that a diagram is scientifically sound or that a template's original objects survived. Cite the original assets and exact evidence, not these design references, for scientific claims.
